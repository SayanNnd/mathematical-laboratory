THE FOUNDATION

the secant line snaps into the tangent.

A straight line connecting two distinct points on a curve is a secant line. Its slope is the average rate of change:

m_secant = [f(a + h) - f(a)] / h

As the distance h contracts toward zero, the second point glides smoothly into the first. The secant pivots and locks into the tangent line, whose slope represents the instantaneous rate of change — the exact derivative f'(a).

THE CASCADE

derivatives of derivatives:
the hierarchy of change.

Differentiation is recursive. If f(x) represents the displacement of a moving particle:

f(t) : Position
f'(t) : Velocity (instantaneous slope)
f''(t) : Acceleration (concavity & curve bending)
f'''(t) : Jerk (rate of change of acceleration)

By stacking derivative tiers, the abstract chain of higher-order rates becomes an intuitive geometric reality: each tier's rotating tangent line paints the next curve beneath it.
